## (2) Verdict by charge

### (a) Is z_lo=2.4 ENOUGH, or should it match the paper's z<2.8?

**Statistically z_lo=2.4 is more than enough; on DATA-PROVENANCE grounds I want z<2.8 (the published cut).**
Two distinct criteria are being conflated:

- **Closure-bias criterion (statistical):** the −0.65σ is carried 86% by z=2.0 (59%) + z=2.2 (27%);
  z≥2.4 KS contributes ≈0 (`nsbias_z_attribution`: z=2.4 +0.009σ, z=2.6 −0.001σ). The kscut scan confirms
  the bias is *already gone* at z_lo=2.1 and is flat-clean for every z_lo≥2.3. So for the *closure metric*,
  z_lo=2.4 is sufficient — dropping z=2.0+2.2 is what matters.
- **Data-quality criterion (physical):** the rationale for the cut is **DLA-finder incompleteness in the
  KODIAQ-SQUAD sample**, and the published KODIAQ-SQUAD analysis (the PI's own paper, cited in the
  docstring) excludes **z<2.8**. The incompleteness does not switch off cleanly at z=2.4: DLA/sub-DLA
  identification degrades continuously toward low z (lower S/N, fewer pixels, the Lyα forest shrinks into
  the atmospheric cutoff), so z=2.4 and z=2.6 KS are *also* compromised by the same systematic that
  motivated dropping z<2.8 — they just don't happen to bias *this particular n_s closure* because the
  closure is an LF-vs-LF self-consistency test, not a data-quality test.

These two criteria can disagree precisely because the closure cannot see a data systematic that is common
to LF-emulator-and-KS-data alike (the closure replaces the data with the emulator's own prediction). **The
honest cut is the one the published analysis used: z<2.8.** Choosing z_lo=2.4 because that is the minimal
cut that passes the *closure* gate is optimizing the wrong objective — it lets z=2.4+2.6 KS, which the PI's
paper deems unreliable, into the *real* likelihood. The cost of z<2.8 over z<2.4 is two z-bins of HR
high-k data; the kscut scan shows the closure is identically clean either way (z_lo=2.8 all-folds would be
≈+0.05σ, same gate). **Recommendation: set `load_ks_leg z_lo=2.8` to match the published KODIAQ-SQUAD cut,
not z_lo=2.4.** This is IMPORTANT, not CRITICAL: it does not change cosmology (DESI carries the low-z
constraint), but it is the difference between "cut tuned to pass closure" and "cut justified by the data
paper" — and a referee will ask exactly this.

**There is also a live internal INCONSISTENCY the resolution must clean up:** the plan's RESOLVED section
(`2026-06-08-ns-bias-rootcause-diagnostics-plan.md` §RESOLVED) states the implemented default is
**z_lo=2.8** ("Dropping KS z<2.8 … `load_ks_leg z_lo=2.8`"), and the docstring of `load_ks_leg` itself
says "the KODIAQ-SQUAD analysis excludes z<2.8" — yet the code defaults to **z_lo=2.4**. The docstring then
claims "dropping z<2.4 removes it (→ +0.04σ)". So the code, its own docstring's stated rationale, and the
plan disagree on whether the cut is 2.4 or 2.8. Adopting z_lo=2.8 resolves all three at once.

### (b) TENSION: PRIYA convergence worst at HeII reion z~3–4, but the LF−HR deficit is worst at LOW z (2.0–2.2) — why?

This is the sharpest Lyα question and I dug into it. **The tension is real as stated but resolves once the
two "worst at" statements are recognized as DIFFERENT objects, and the low-z deficit is a genuine physical
resolution effect (not mean-flux/thermal contamination).** My evidence:

1. **The deficit is NOT a coordinate artifact.** I checked: a fixed velocity k [s/km] maps to comoving k
   [h/Mpc] by ×H(z)/(1+z), which is *smallest at low z* (66 at z=2 vs 87 at z=4.6 km/s/Mpc). So at fixed
   k=0.069 s/km the comoving scale is LARGER (more converged) at low z — the coordinate effect predicts the
   deficit should be *milder* at low z, the OPPOSITE of what we see. So the worse-at-low-z is physical, not
   a k-axis convention.

2. **The deficit is NOT a mean-flux mismatch** (check (2)-2 above: ⟨F⟩ matched to <0.15%).

3. **What PRIYA actually says "worst at z~3–4" is about a different decomposition.** Per
   `reference-priya-sims`, PRIYA's "convergence worst during He II reionization z≈4→3.1" describes the
   behavior of the **multi-fidelity GP correction `ρ(k,z)·P_LF + δ`** in its published k-range and its δ
   term, in the context of the *overall* convergence budget that is sub-% for z≤2.6. It is a statement
   about where the suite's quoted total error peaks, dominated by the thermal/IGM-state sensitivity around
   HeII reionization, evaluated over their reported band. The object **I** am measuring is the raw
   clean-forest LF→HR ratio at **high k near the LF Nyquist (k>0.04 s/km, up to 0.069)** — the small-scale
   tail. These need not peak at the same z.

4. **The physical reason the high-k LF→HR deficit peaks at LOW z is the forest transmissivity / small-scale
   power budget.** At z=2 the forest is most transmissive (⟨F⟩≈0.88; my check) so the P1D high-k power is
   dominated by the thinnest, least-saturated, smallest-scale density/velocity structure in voids and
   filaments — exactly the structure most sensitive to SPH particle resolution and pressure (Jeans)
   smoothing. As z rises and the forest saturates (⟨F⟩→0.46 by z=4), absorption washes out the small-scale
   modes (saturated regions carry no high-k information), so the LF's small-scale deficiency matters less.
   The α-rung split is the clinching evidence: at FIXED z=2.0–2.2, going to LOWER τ₀ / HIGHER ⟨F⟩ (more
   transmissive) makes the high-k deficit WORSE (−8.5% vs −4.8%). Transmissivity drives the deficit — and
   transmissivity is highest at low z. That is a coherent, self-consistent IGM-physics story.

5. **Caveat I flag (does NOT change the verdict):** my measurement also disagrees with Khan+2023's
   velocity-field picture (their residual is *largest at high z*, ~50% z=5 → percent z=2), but Khan is
   256³–1024³ grid-IC at 20 h⁻¹Mpc and is about IC/particle-initialization, not n1536→n3072 box convergence
   at 120 h⁻¹Mpc — a different regime (`reference-khan2023`), so I do not treat the disagreement as
   disqualifying. It does mean the worse-at-low-z trend is NOT independently corroborated by the cited
   literature, so I would not over-claim it as "expected"; I'd call it "physically plausible and internally
   self-consistent (transmissivity-driven), measured cleanly, but z-trend not externally cross-checked."

**Bottom line on (b): no contradiction — different objects; the low-z deficit is a genuine,
transmissivity-driven small-scale resolution effect, cleanly separated from mean flux.** It is exactly the
regime an LF-only emulator carries into the real fit, so the MF fix is well-targeted.

### (c) Does MF / HR (3072³) actually resolve the low-z small-scale physics driving the −6%?

**Partially, and this is the one place I want a hard caveat rather than a clean GO.** HR at 3072³ (39 kpc/h)
is 2× the LF linear resolution and is the best available, and the LF→HR ratio is the standard PRIYA
correction object, so wiring it in is correct and necessary. BUT:

- **HR is not the converged truth, only a better rung.** PRIYA's own L15 384³/512³ small-box diagnostic
  (the `res_corr` table in `multifidelity.py`, ≈7% at k=0.1) exists precisely because even 3072³ is not
  asymptotically converged at the top of the band, especially at the most-stringent z. The MF forward
  *already* applies `res_corr` ON TOP of `ρ`/`gbar`, which is the right structure — but the residual
  HR-non-convergence at low-z high-k is itself worst exactly where the LF→HR deficit is worst (low z), and
  there is no n6144 to LOSO it. So "MF resolves it" should be "MF removes the LF→HR piece (−6%) and applies
  the separately-calibrated L15 particle-convergence factor; a residual HR-non-convergence at low-z
  near-Nyquist remains and must live in a C_emu floor." The MF plan's small-scale-leg C_emu floor (~0.9%
  LOSO residual) is the right vehicle, but it is sized from the LF→HR LOSO, not from any HR→truth test, so
  it does not bound HR's own non-convergence. **I would explicitly state that the C_emu floor covers LF→HR
  generalization but NOT HR→truth, and lean on the k<0.06 analysis cap (which keeps us below the worst
  near-Nyquist HR non-convergence) as the actual protection.**

- The good news: the analysis is capped at k<0.06 (memory `phase2-analysis-scope-dla`), and the LF→HR
  deficit at z=2.0, k=0.06 is the −6 to −8% being corrected; the residual HR non-convergence at k<0.06 is
  expected to be the percent-level PRIYA quotes for k<0.05. So the cap does most of the protective work.

### (d) Is the τ₀(mean-flux)-dependence of the correction physically expected?

**Yes, and it is correctly the second axis of the planned z+τ₀-resolved correction.** The ρ correction
depending on mean flux is physically expected: the high-k P1D is shaped by which density structures are in
the unsaturated (information-carrying) regime, and that is set by ⟨F⟩ (=τ₀). Lower τ₀ / higher ⟨F⟩ exposes
more small-scale structure to the P1D, so the LF's small-scale deficiency bites harder — exactly the sign I
measured (low-τ₀ deficit −8.5% vs high-τ₀ −4.8% at z=2.0–2.2 high-k; `mf_rescorr_vs_tau0_z` ~9pp swing).
A PURELY geometric resolution factor (independent of the gas state) would be τ₀-independent; the observed
τ₀-dependence says the correction couples to the thermal/ionization state, which is physically right and is
why the plan's move from an α-pooled `gbar(z,k)` to a **z+τ₀-resolved** correction is correct and necessary
— the current `FixedMeanHead`/`fixed_mean_table` pools over all α at fixed z (`multifidelity.py:643-669`),
which averages away a real ~9pp (in P) / ~4.3pp (in ρ) τ₀ trend. Pooling it leaves a τ₀-correlated residual
that, because Kim τ rises with z, partially aliases into the z-trend AND into a coherent n_s-direction
residual at the low-z high-k corner. **Endorse the z+τ₀-resolved correction; it is the physically-motivated
fix.**
